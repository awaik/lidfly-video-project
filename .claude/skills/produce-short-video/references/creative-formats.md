# Reusable short-video formats

These are planning patterns, not rigid prompt templates. Select one dominant format and adapt it to the brief.

## 1. Reveal hook

- Open on a visual contradiction or result before explaining it.
- Hold enough information back to create one clear question.
- Pay off the exact question before the CTA.
- Route the opening shot toward composition control; do not bury the hook in a long establishing shot.

## 2. Product launch

- Problem state -> product interaction -> visible outcome -> proof/CTA.
- Keep product geometry and labels in a reference sheet.
- Generate product plates and human interaction separately when contact or logo integrity is risky.
- Add exact UI, pricing, labels, and CTA in post.

## 3. Founder or expert voice

- One precise claim, one lived detail, one proof point, one invitation.
- Prefer short dialogue shots with room for cutaways.
- Capture neutral reaction and listening shots to hide lip-sync edits.
- Do not fabricate credentials, outcomes, or customer evidence.

## 4. Course or framework promo

- Show the painful before-state, name the mechanism, demonstrate one useful step, then offer the full path.
- Put diagrams, steps, and exact copy in post-production graphics.
- Use generated visuals as demonstrations, metaphors, or background plates.

## 5. Faceless explainer

- Let narration carry logic while images carry examples and rhythm.
- Design shot-to-shot visual motifs so the sequence feels authored.
- Avoid random stock-like cuts that merely repeat nouns from the voice-over.
- Mix establishing, process, detail, and payoff shots.

## 6. Premium aesthetic

- Use restraint: controlled camera, few materials, coherent palette, and deliberate negative space.
- Anchor product proportions and surface behavior with references.
- Prefer fewer high-quality shots over dense multi-shot prompting.
- Let sound design and edit cadence create much of the perceived value.

## 7. Before and after

- Make both states measurable and compositionally comparable.
- Use locked or matched framing when the change itself is the evidence.
- Preserve lighting and camera geometry unless their change is intentional.
- Do not imply unsupported real-world results.

## 8. Testimonial story

- Context -> specific obstacle -> decision -> observable change -> bounded recommendation.
- Treat names, quotes, likeness, and claims as rights/fact-check gates.
- Use a real approved testimonial verbatim or label a fictional dramatization clearly.
- Keep generated B-roll subordinate to the evidence.

## 9. Avatar-led clip

- Establish identity and voice permissions before generation.
- Build several clean identity and wardrobe references.
- Keep spoken beats short and cover transitions with cutaways.
- Review mouth shapes, teeth, eye direction, hands, and vocal consistency.

## 10. Podcast visualizer

- Preserve the real audio as the source of truth.
- Use generated scenes as illustrative cutaways, not fabricated speaker footage unless authorized and disclosed.
- Design recurring visual chapters, waveform/caption treatments, and reaction beats in the edit.
- Keep speaker labels, quotes, and timestamps deterministic.

## Timing pattern

For a typical short, assign time by function rather than by a fixed formula:

```text
hook -> context -> escalation/demonstration -> payoff -> CTA or loop
```

Every shot must earn its duration by changing information, emotion, or state. If it does none of those, cut it before generation.
